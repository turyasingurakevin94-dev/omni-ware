This screen, and Creditors

Here the unit is one bill: what a supplier billed you on one day, and whether it is right. On Creditors the unit is one supplier: what you owe them altogether, and who to pay first.

A payment recorded on either shows on both. Nothing is counted twice, and nothing is decided in two places.

What is checked

Every bill in the range, every time this screen is drawn: the same delivery billed twice; a line priced above anything you have ever paid that supplier; a bill with no delivery behind it on the stock record; and money paid out beyond what was billed.

Not checked, because nothing on file can answer it: whether the goods that came were the ones ordered, and whether a price you have never paid before is a fair one. For that second question, Compare prices.

What voiding does

On a bill raised from one of your own orders to a supplier, voiding is not a flag — the goods come back off the shelf and the order goes back to waiting. On one raised from a customer's order it drops what is owed and leaves the stock alone.

The button says which of the two it is about to do before it does it, and a bill with money against it cannot be voided at all.
