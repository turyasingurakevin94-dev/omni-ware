Why, for whoever reads this next

Every sheet is fixed with eight nails — two per purlin, four purlins. Anybody who takes sheets and no nails is coming back tomorrow, so ask at the counter.

The five verbs, and they are the whole vocabulary. Needs — it cannot be used without it, and the picture says so. Goes with — usually bought together, offered but not insisted on. Instead of — a swap when the first is out of stock or too dear. Runs out after — it will be finished in about so many days, and the picture comes back then. Part of — both belong to one job, like roofing or plumbing.

These are pairs that keep turning up on the same invoice. The books can see WHAT happens together; only you know WHY, and whether it is a rule or a coincidence. Saying no is as useful as saying keep — it stops being offered.

What a pairing lets a picture say

With a figure: the third frame carries a quantity worked from what they are buying — 40 bags becomes 25 kg of nails. That is the frame that reads as a hardware man rather than an advert.

Without a figure: the frame names the thing and shows its photo, and no number appears. Nothing is invented to fill the gap.

Never: a pairing to something out of stock, to a line with no price, or to a product with no photo — the frame is dropped and the picture is three frames instead of four.
