What the old matcher would have done

Nothing. It scored a product by how much of its OWN NAME the message covered — four words of five is 0.8, and only a perfect 1.0 answers by itself. So the customer who asks precisely got silence, while a one-word product like cement scored a perfect 1.0 on any message containing the word and answered at once. Short names won; precise questions lost.

Scoring both ways fixes it. How much of their message this product explains stops a one-word product claiming a long question; how much of its name they said stops a vague question claiming a specific product. A reply goes out on its own only when the second is whole and the first has nothing left over.
