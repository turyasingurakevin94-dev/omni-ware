Why a table

Twenty-four stacked cards is 9,800 px of scroll. Twenty-four rows is 872, and you can read the whole set without moving. The card carried a SKU, a photo picker and two markup fields that are blank on 22 of the 24 — a column can say "default" in one word where a card needed a labelled input.

What the eye gets for free

The two rows that were touched are marked by shape — an inset steel rule and a tinted ground — so "which of these has its own markup?" is answered at a glance rather than by reading twenty-four cards. Figures are tabular mono and right-aligned, so the markup columns line up and a wrong one shows up as a ragged edge.

The step, and the cost of it

The modal widens from 720 to 1160 between the steps. That is a real cost — the dialog moves under you — and it buys a table that does not need horizontal scrolling at 1440. A simple product never sees it: for one item there is only step one, and the button says Save, not Next.
