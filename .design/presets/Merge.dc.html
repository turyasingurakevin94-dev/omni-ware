One at a time, each with its own count. A “merge all duplicates” button would move 29 products on one click, and two of these three are a judgement rather than an obvious tidy-up: a wall plug under Gypsum may genuinely be a different item from one under Furniture.

What this will not do

It will not merge a category into a sub-category. The direction is always toward the shallower word, because a category can hold sub-categories and a sub-category cannot hold anything.

It will not delete a word that is still on a product. Deleting a word from a list only stops it being suggested; merging is the only thing that touches a record, and it moves rather than removes.

It is one action, and it can be undone. The toast carries Undo for as long as the screen is open, and the merge is written as one change so undoing it puts all 6 back where they were.

The catch-all is not a merge

Furniture’s 39 sub-categories are a different problem, and the sheet above cannot solve it: nothing here can decide that a bow saw belongs under Tools rather than Furniture. What the page offers instead is Move to another category on a selection of sub-category chips — you pick the ones that are tools, pick where they go, and the products follow. Same counting, same undo, your judgement.
