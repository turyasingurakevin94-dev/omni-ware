THE JOB OF THIS SCREEN

One owner, at the desk, working a list down in a sitting until it is empty. The unit of work is one message to one client — never one row per item, because nobody sends somebody three WhatsApps in a row.

So the screen is a queue on the left and the message on the right. Choose a client, read why they are on the list, read the words that will actually reach them, send, and the row leaves.

Everything here is drawn from index.html's real tokens: the 23-colour palette, the 11/12/13/14 type ramp, Inter + Archivo Black + IBM Plex Mono, the .ow-* console components (.ow-strip, .ow-pan, .ow-lr, .ow-cp, .ow-av, .ow-msg, .ow-bk, .ow-grid-l) and the existing .btn family. No new component, no new colour, no fourteenth button.

WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE SCREEN AS IT STANDS →

1. A card wall, not a console. At 14 clients this is already 2,400px of scrolling; at 40 it is unusable at a desk.

2. The accent is everywhere. Every card carries an oxide "Message on WhatsApp", and every avatar paints oxide too. Forty of them signal nothing. The law is: the accent appears ONCE per screen.

3. .fup-avatar fails contrast outright — #B23A26 on #14171B is about 2.7:1 at 14px bold. The house .ow-av (steel ground, ink-600) exists for exactly this.

4. The message is invisible until it is gone. You press a button, a tab opens, and only then do you see what you are about to send to a client. There is no reading it and no editing it.

5. "I told them" sits beside "Message" as a peer. It silences every reason for that client and can be pressed without anything ever being sent.

6. Off-system values: radius 11px, sizes 14.5/13.5/12.5/11.5/10px, weights 650 and 800 that the app does not load, and five raw near-whites.

7. Nothing truncates. "Iron sheets — G28, 3m box profile" has no ellipsis on any of the three name classes.

8. No triage. Forty clients arrive as one undifferentiated column with no way to see what the news actually is.

THE PROPOSAL — what changed and why

The strip names the work instead of counting curiosities. "Clients to message / Being watched / Never followed up" became the five things the code already knows: to message, goods arrived, sourcing moved, price changed, gone quiet. It is a triage line — it says what today's work IS, not merely how much of it there is.

The queue is .ow-lr rows at 44px. Each carries the client, the dominant reason as a chip, one line of meta, and the one honest figure this screen has: days since anything was said. Tabular, right-aligned, and it is the sort key — so the column reads down as a ranking rather than as decoration.

The work pane is the client being worked. The three things they are waiting on sit here, with the full reason and a quiet per-item Close — visible for ONE client rather than for all forty.

The message is on the screen. .ow-msg, editable, addressed, with the app's standing promise underneath: nothing has been sent. "Send on WhatsApp" is the single oxide element on the whole page — it is the act the owner came here to do.

"Price changed" split into "Price down" (verdigris) and "Price up" (amber). followUpPriceMoved already computes `down`; the old screen threw that away and painted both amber, telling the owner that good news was a caution.

THE SECOND TAB — the register

The "To contact" tab is work. This one is housekeeping, so it is denser and carries no accent at all: nothing here is an act.

A 34px table row, grouped by client (the unit the shop thinks in), with the state, when it was asked, when they were last told, and Close/Reopen. Open clients first; a client whose list is entirely settled is history, not work.

The search moved into the panel head and gained a state filter — at 38 open and 112 closed, "Open / Closed / All" is the difference between a register and an archive you cannot get out of.

Closed rows keep their strike-through but drop to ink-400 on paper-2, so history reads as history without a colour being spent on it.

THE EMPTY STATE

"Not enough is an answer." A quiet screen must be told apart from a screen that failed to load, so it says what it is watching and names what would put a client on the list.

Under it, the three closest to going quiet — nothing to do, but it proves the machinery is running and gives the day a shape. Derived from the same quiet-days setting in the header, never invented.

THE PHONE IS ITS OWN DESIGN

820px is a switch, not a reflow. The desk gets a two-column console; the phone gets a queue of cards and a full-screen sheet, and neither is a squeezed version of the other.

Body type 15px, card padding 14–16, page padding 16, the metric strip folded to 2×2 in one card with hairline dividers and no per-tile borders.

The cards carry no buttons. Tapping one opens the sheet, where the single oxide "Send on WhatsApp" lives at 48px in the thumb zone.

The list's one accent is the dark sticky bar above the tab bar: "Start with Ssekitoleko Hardware". It is the one thing to do next, and it sits where the thumb already is.

No fake status bar and no fake keyboard — the real ones render on top of this.

Tab bar: active is FILLED, inactive is stroked. A shape change reads at arm's length in daylight where a colour change does not.
