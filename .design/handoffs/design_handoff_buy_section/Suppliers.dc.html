The cut: Creditors becomes the You owe lens
The rail draws the final map: Sell 6, Buy 2, Catalogue 4, Money 4, Insight 4, Setup 3 — 23 rows and Today, from 33 and Today. Every cut is argued in Rail.dc.html, which is the authority if a frame disagrees.
The Creditors screen is this list with one column showing. Its own test says so in as many words — the Creditors List is built by mapping suppliers — and the balance it shows is creditorTotalOwed(id), read off the same purchase invoices this screen reads. Two renderings of one figure, and the app has already been bitten by it: deleting a supplier left Creditors totalling nothing while the bill was still on the books, which is why deleteSupplier now refuses while anything is owed.
What is lost
Nothing. Creditors ranked by amount; the You owe lens groups by whether they are also late delivering, which is the only thing that changes what you do about a bill.
What absorbs it
This screen, with the oldest-first bill list and allocateCreditorPayment behind the Pay button. Debtors went to Customers for the same reason; this is the same cut on the buy side.
The words still reach it
"creditors", "who you owe", "payables", "bills" and "supplier balance" become Suppliers keywords — the label keeps its own word, as the rail's rule-9 note requires. resolveTab('analytics-creditors') opens Suppliers on You owe.
Three readings this screen adds: says · keeps — the promised lead time against the one the supplier actually holds, which is the figure the buy plan uses and the reason Ndeeba's 4 days became 8. Price drift, six months of it per supplier and per line: Roto put the runners up 15% while your own price stood still, which is the entire explanation of that line's 5% and the argument the Manager's hold is making. And concentration — 78% of your cement comes from one supplier who is also the only one quoting tiers, so the discount and the risk are the same fact.
