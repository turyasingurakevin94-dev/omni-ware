The cut: Compare prices stops being a screen
The rail draws the final map: Sell 6, Buy 2, Catalogue 4, Money 4, Insight 4, Setup 3 — 23 rows and Today, from 33 and Today. Every cut is argued in Rail.dc.html, which is the authority if a frame disagrees.
Buy holds three rows — compare, sourcing, suppliers — and Compare is the one to go. Every line on this plan has already ranked the suppliers for the quantity it recommends and named the next rung down that supplier's own ladder; the price-rule editor already argues shelf cost against the cheapest quote; and Sourcing picks the supplier for a line that has a quantity on it. A standalone compare screen re-does that reading without a quantity — and the price depends on the quantity, so it compares a number nobody will pay.
What is lost
A side-by-side table of every supplier's price for one product. It returns as the row's own supplier line, the tier prompt, and the full ladder when you open the line.
What absorbs it
Sourcing, the plan line and the price-rule editor — all three of which know the quantity. Suppliers keeps its row: who you buy from and how late they run is a relationship, not a price list. Buy goes from three rows to two.
The words still reach it
"compare", "cheapest", "price list" and "quotes" land on Sourcing; resolveTab('compare') opens Sourcing's line picker, which asks for a quantity first — the same pattern as 'buying' → Forecasts/Stock. "Purchase order", "on order" and "holds" arm the lenses here.
Four things the screen now says out loud: the rank is shillings earned, so a 5%-margin line that turns over hard can honestly sit above a 38% one — and the kept share sits on the row so a thin one is visible without a meeting. A hold argues, it never hides: the held line keeps its rank and its Buy it button, with the Manager's own sentence above the instruction rather than below it. The basket is measured against what is safe to spend, not the balance, and names the line whose cover could wait. And the lead time the plan uses is the one the supplier actually keeps — Ndeeba Timber's 4 days became 8 after three late deliveries. Open orders and standing holds are panels on this screen rather than pages, for the same reason: the plan subtracts what is already coming before it recommends anything, so a second screen drawing those rows can only disagree with it.
