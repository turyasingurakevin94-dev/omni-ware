The cut: WhatsApp and Follow-ups become Messages
The rail draws the final map: Sell 6, Buy 2, Catalogue 4, Money 4, Insight 4, Setup 3 — 23 rows and Today, from 33 and Today. Every cut is argued in Rail.dc.html, which is the authority if a frame disagrees.
Follow-ups is a list of people you owe a word, already tagged with a reason — money, telling them something. WhatsApp is the box you write that word in. Neither is usable without the other: the list's only action is to open the box, and the box's only content comes from the list. Two rows in the rail, one job, and a search problem the code already documents — the note in the nav index says the obvious search for Follow-ups is "broadcast", while broadcast is WhatsApp's screen. Nobody can be expected to know which door to use.
What is lost
Nothing. Templates and broadcast were WhatsApp's own two features: broadcast is a button in the top bar, and a template is what the draft box is pre-filled with.
What absorbs it
One screen: the outbox grouped by reason on the left, the actual draft on the right. One rail row, one badge — navBadgeFollowUps already counts the whole thing.
The words still reach it
"whatsapp", "broadcast", "chase", "remind", "follow up" and "message" all land on Messages, and the existing aliases — 'chase' → money, 'telling' → telling — become the group they scroll to.
The honesty the code insists on, made visible: the app cannot see WhatsApp. The draft is editable because the box is what ships, and after the hand-off you stamp it was sent or it was not — nothing is recorded until you do. That constraint is also where the insight lives: across 184 sent messages, itemising the goods and asking for a date each roughly double the reply rate, and five chases with two broken promises says messages have stopped working on that account.
