The cut: one ranking of what is running out, not two
The rail draws the final map: Sell 6, Buy 2, Catalogue 4, Money 4, Insight 4, Setup 3 — 23 rows and Today, from 33 and Today. Every cut is argued in Rail.dc.html, which is the authority if a frame disagrees. No screen is cut in Catalogue this turn — Movements is already a lens here, absorbed before this pass began, and the log on the panel is that lens in miniature.
What is duplicated here is a judgement, not a page. The below-floor queue ranks lines by how soon each runs out at the rate it has actually sold — and the buy plan ranks the same lines, off the same restockRiskRows(), by what they earn. Two rankings of one set of lines, and a shop working down the wrong one buys its thinnest lines first.
What changes
Inventory counts what is under its floor and says how many are already on the plan — 7 lines · 3 already on the buy plan — and hands the ordering decision over. It never ranks a buy.
What is lost
Nothing. The days-left figure stays on the row, where it explains the floor; the 7-line queue becomes a link into Forecasts · Stock with those lines shown.
Why this way round
The plan already reads cash, supplier lead times and what is on the way. A floor cannot see any of that, so a floor should raise the question and the plan should answer it.
Three rules the code already keeps, now visible on the screen: null is not zero — Murram reads cannot be valued, not 0, and sorts last rather than as worthless, because "nobody wrote down what it cost" and "worth nothing" are different facts. The strip is the whole shelf and says so — 48,600,000 at what you paid, 192 lines on file, never moving when you filter — while the list header counts what is listed under it, 12 lines and 9,240,000. And no tile ever reads zero: a count that would be nought is replaced by the reading that makes it good news, so a healthy shelf never looks like a broken app. Photographs sit on the line itself, dropped once and reused by the price card and the WhatsApp post — which is the argument the Media screen will have to answer, since a gallery of pictures divorced from the products they belong to cannot tell you that 61 lines have none.
