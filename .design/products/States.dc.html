Products

The four states the register has to be able to say out loud.

1Nothing in the register yetNo strip of four zeros, because four figures of zero are four figures that mean nothing.

Nothing in the register yet

A product is a thing you sell: a name, a category, and the markup rule that turns a supplier’s price into your own. Everything else in the app hangs off it — a supplier quote is a quote for a product, and so is a stock count, an order line and a row on the printed catalogue.

Nothing else appears on this screen until there is something to count.

No supplier priceNobody has quoted it, so there is no cost to mark up. Cleared by one price in the registry, or by receiving a delivery that carries a cost.
No markup ruleIt has a cost but nothing to turn it into a price — no rule of its own, and no shop default behind it. Cleared in the line, or once for the whole shop.
No variants builtSold in options that do not exist yet, so there is nothing to price, stock or sell. Cleared by building the options in the line’s record.
No photo — and not a faultIt sells perfectly well without one. Only the printed catalogue and the item picker are poorer for it, so it is counted in the strip and never marked on the line.

The fourth line is the one that matters: a missing photo is not a fault. It is counted in the strip and never chipped on a row — which is what keeps the amber worth reading, because nineteen of thirty-one lines have no photo and only four are genuinely unfinished.

The name column gives way and says so with an ellipsis, carrying the whole value in its tooltip; the basis line under the figure does the same. No money column ever truncates. “1,240,00” is a tenth of “1,240,000” and looks entirely plausible — a clipped figure is not a shortened figure, it is a wrong one. The same rule bars a bare <input> from holding a value that can outgrow it: an input clips mid-glyph with no ellipsis and no tooltip, so “Mulongo Hardware” reads as a different supplier rather than as a shortened one.

Your price book did not load, so this register cannot say what anything costs or fetches. The names, IDs and categories below are current — those read fine. Every money column says not read rather than 0, because a zero here would claim a line is free. The set-up count is switched off rather than shown short: it cannot know what is unfinished without the prices, and a count that quietly leaves lines out is worse than no count.
